# TaskForce AI: live demo speech flow (Dixie Road & Mayfield Road)

For the presenter, live in front of a COO. About 8–10 minutes plus Q&A.

**The rule:** the reports are built live, so their numbers and charts change from run to run. Tell the story; let the
report carry the numbers. Never say a figure before it is on screen. Point at it, then read it.

---

## Before you start
- Logged in to TaskForce (staging), TF AI open, microphone allowed.
- Each question in a **new chat** (keeps the reports clean).
- The DSR is a fresh report for today: don't open an old draft.
- Have the PDF download and the share icons visible on the first report (you'll point at them once).

---

## 1. The story (about 1 minute, no screen yet)

> Picture this. You're running the Dixie Road and Mayfield Road widening. Two roads, crews out every day, the Region
> watching. It's Monday, seven a.m.
>
> Over the weekend, the updates piled up. Daily site reports from the field. A schedule that moved. Costs landing in
> finance. Extra work waiting on the client's sign-off.
>
> Every one of them matters. None of them talk to each other. And the one question you actually have, *are we okay*,
> isn't answered in any of them.
>
> So you start calling. The project manager, the superintendent, accounting. By Thursday you have an answer. By then,
> it's already cost you.

*(Pause. Then open TF AI.)*

> Now imagine you just ask. TaskForce AI goes through every one of those updates for you, and comes back with one page:
> what's wrong, how bad, and what to do first. Every number on it comes straight from your own records.

---

## 2. Monday, 7 a.m.: where do we stand?

**Say:** "So. Monday, seven a.m. One question."

**Ask:**
> What should I worry about on Dixie this week?

**While it works** (about 30 s; the steps scroll on screen):
> Watch what it's doing: it's going through the schedule, the site reports, the money, the delays, all at once. This is
> the part that normally takes your team three days.

**When the page lands, point at:**
1. **The top: "What's wrong / How bad / Do this first."** "That's the whole Monday meeting in three lines."
2. **The tiles.** Read them as they are.
3. **The delay chart.** "Half of what's holding this job up came from outside: other parties, not our crews."
4. **The map at the bottom.** "And it knows where the job is: Dixie Road and Mayfield Road, from the survey."

**Bridge:** "One page, from the live records, instead of three reports from three departments. Let's pull each thread."

---

## 3. The leak nobody reports: idle crews

**Say:** "First, the leak nobody puts in a report: crews being paid to wait."

**Ask:**
> Where are we paying for crews that aren't producing on Dixie?

**When the page lands, point at:**
1. **Standby by trade.** Read the top two trades and their hours.
2. **The monthly trend.** "And here's when it happened": name the peak month on the chart.
3. **The daily reports carrying the most standby.** "These are the actual days. That's where the conversation with the
   superintendent starts this week."

**The point:** "Nobody hid this. It was sitting in the site reports. Nobody had time to add it up."

---

## 4. The money: what are we owed?

**Say:** "Now the money."

**Ask:**
> How much money is the Dixie & Mayfield job owed right now, and what should we chase first?

**When the page lands, point at:**
1. **The headline figure** ("money stuck"). Read it. "Most of that is work already in the ground, not yet approved for
   billing."
2. **The items chart.** "And it tells you which line to chase first": read the top item and its amount.
3. **"Do this first."** "That goes in this month's progress claim."
4. **The share icons / Download PDF.** "And this page goes straight to your project manager." (Point, don't click.)

**The point:** "That's working capital sitting on site. Finding it took one question."

---

## 5. The delays: what can we bill back?

**Say:** "Now the delays, and which of them belong on a claim."

**Ask:**
> Which Dixie delays can we bill back to the Region?

**When the page lands, point at:**
1. **The delay table.** Read two or three descriptions: e.g. "an unmarked Bell duct bank, nesting birds, an
   archaeological find."
2. **Each row's report and date.** "Every one logged by date and by report. That's the evidence a claim is built on."
3. **The map.** "And here's where it's happening."

**Careful:** say "the ones to take to the Region", not "we will recover X". The claim is the team's call.

---

## 6. The field: the daily report, by voice

**Say:**
> Every answer you just saw is only as good as what comes in from site. So here's what a site supervisor does at the
> end of his day.

**Ask (or say):**
> I want to fill today's daily site report.

**The form opens beside the chat. Speak these lines, one at a time, pausing until the form fills (about 2 s each):**

1. "Start a daily report for Dixie Road and Mayfield Road, on the Dixie corridor site. Day shift, Gurpreet Sandhu
   supervising."
   *(TF AI asks for the weather once. Ignore it; keep going.)*
2. "On the storm sewer we had one open-cut foreman, four open-cut labourers and two pipelayers, ten hours each."
3. "We ran the 40 tonne excavator and three tri-axle dump trucks, ten hours each."
4. "On item C-15 a we placed three hundred tonnes."
5. "We lost two hours waiting for Bell to locate a duct bank at station fourteen plus two-eighty."
6. **"What's still missing?"**
   *(TF AI: "Still empty: Weather, Temperature." and the form jumps to the weather.)*
   **Say:** "It checks what's left, and takes you straight to it."
7. "Partly cloudy, twelve degrees."

**Point at:** the crew, the equipment, the quantity against the contract item, the Bell delay, all filled in.

> That two-hour Bell delay is now on the record, at a station, the same day. That's the paper trail that wins a delay
> claim. And these hours and quantities feed tomorrow's standby and billing answers.

*(Don't save the report in front of them: close it.)*

**Words to keep exactly** (otherwise it stops to ask "which one?"): "open-cut foreman", "open-cut labourers",
"On item C-15 a", "40 tonne excavator". Leave the weather out of line 1.

---

## 7. The close (30 seconds)

> Know where a job stands in one minute, not one week.
> Find idle crews and unbilled work while it's still recoverable.
> Every delay logged the same day it happens, ready for a claim.
>
> You don't need more reports. You need fewer steps between the question and the answer.

---

## If something goes off-script
| What happens | What to do |
|---|---|
| A report takes over a minute | "It's reading every record on this job, not a summary." Keep talking about the problem it solves. |
| TF AI asks a question back with options | Pick the obvious one by number ("one"). Say: "When it's not sure, it asks, it doesn't guess." |
| A chart shows something odd (e.g. a very old milestone) | Don't read it. Move to "Do this first." |
| The DSR asks "which trade?" or "which material?" | Answer with the number. Say: "It matches what you say to your real lists." |
| Numbers differ from a previous run | Normal: the records are live. Never promise a number before it's on screen. |

## Q&A they're likely to ask
- **"How do I know the numbers are right?"** Every figure is counted from your own records, and each page lists where
  it came from (site reports, schedule, finance, contract). The AI writes the words, not the numbers.
- **"What does it take to get our jobs in?"** If your jobs are in TaskForce, it works on them as they are: no separate
  data project. It's only as good as the daily reports, which is why the field part takes a minute by voice.
- **"Who sees what, and where does our data go?"** Don't improvise: "We'll send you a one-page answer on access and
  data handling."
