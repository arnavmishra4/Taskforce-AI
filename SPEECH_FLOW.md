# TaskForce AI: the speech (Dixie Road & Mayfield Road)

*Read it straight through. Lines in quotes inside the speech are typed or said to TaskForce AI as you say them. The
reports are built live, so never say a number until it is on the screen: point, then read.*

---

Picture this. You're running the Dixie Road and Mayfield Road widening. Two roads, crews out every day, the Region
watching. It's Monday, seven a.m.

Over the weekend, the updates piled up. Daily site reports from the field. A schedule that moved. Costs landing in
finance. Extra work waiting on the client's sign-off.

Every one of them matters. None of them talk to each other. And the one question you actually have, are we okay, isn't
answered in any of them.

So you start calling. The project manager, the superintendent, accounting. By Thursday you have an answer. By then,
it's already cost you.

Now imagine you just ask. TaskForce AI goes through every one of those updates for you, and comes back with one page:
what's wrong, how bad, and what to do first.

And every number on that page comes straight from your own records.

So let's do exactly that. Monday, seven a.m., one question.

**"What should I worry about on Dixie this week?"**

Watch it work. It's going through the schedule, the site reports, the money and the delays, all at once. The part that
normally takes your team a week.

And there it is. What's wrong, how bad, and what to do first: your whole Monday meeting, before your coffee. The tiles
show where the job stands against the plan.

Look at what's waiting on the Region's sign-off. And the map: Dixie Road and Mayfield Road, exactly where the work is.

Talha runs Dixie day to day, and should see this. Just ask.

**"Send this report to Talha Asif"**

**"Yes"**

It found Talha's work email in your staff records. It goes out as a PDF.

One page, instead of three reports from three departments. Now pull each thread. Start with the leak nobody reports:
crews paid to wait.

**"Where are we paying for crews that aren't producing on Dixie?"**

Here it is, in dollars: what we paid crews to stand by, trade by trade.

And where those hours were charged, item by item. Nobody hid this. It sat in the site reports. Nobody had time to add
it up.

Now the money.

**"How much money is the Dixie & Mayfield job owed right now, and what should we chase first?"**

That's what the job is owed right now. The biggest piece is a progress claim the Region has already certified, but not
yet paid. Then the extra work waiting on their sign-off.

The holdback sits on its own: the Region keeps it until the job is done, so it isn't owed yet. And it tells you what
to chase first. Working capital, found with one question.

Now the delays, and which of them belong on a claim.

**"Which Dixie delays can we bill back to the Region?"**

Every delay on this job, logged by the site, and what each one cost: the change order behind it, approved or still
waiting on the Region. An unmarked Bell duct bank. Nesting birds. An archaeological find.

That's the evidence a claim is built on, in dollars. The third-party ones go to the Region; our own rework stays with
us.

And here's where it's all happening.

Every answer you've just seen is only as good as what comes in from site. So here's the other end: a site supervisor
at the end of the day.

**"I want to fill today's daily site report."**

The report opens beside the chat. No typing, no dropdowns. Just talking.

**"Okay, let's start a daily report. This one's for Dixie Road and Mayfield Road, on the Dixie corridor site. It was a
day shift, and the supervisor is Gurpreet Sandhu."**

**"For work progress, on item C-15 a, ninety percent of the Granular A is complete. The remaining ten percent will be
completed by next week."**

**"For labour, on the same item, we had one Foreman Open Cut and four Labourer Open Cut. They each put in ten
hours."**

**"For equipment, on the same item, we had the 40 tonne excavator going for ten hours."**

**"For quantities, on item C-15 a, we placed three hundred tonnes."**

**"For delays, we lost two hours this morning, waiting on Bell to locate a duct bank at station fourteen plus
two-eighty."**

Progress, crew, equipment, the quantity against the contract item, and the Bell delay, each matched to the real
records. Then one more question.

**"Okay, what else is missing?"**

It says what's left, and goes straight to it.

**"For weather, it was partly cloudy, twelve degrees."**

Done. That Bell delay is on the record, at a station, the same day: the paper trail that wins a claim. And these hours
feed tomorrow morning's answers.

So here's what this means for you. Know where a job stands in one minute, not one week. Catch idle crews and unbilled
work while it's recoverable. Log every delay the day it happens.

You don't need more reports. You need fewer steps between the question and the answer. Thank you.
